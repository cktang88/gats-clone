# Tinwar

Tinwar is a top-down multiplayer arena shooter for the browser. Its gameplay is modeled on gats.io. The name, art and code are original. Play it at https://tinwar.io.

## Run

```bash
npm install
npm start
```

Open http://localhost:8080. Set `PORT` to change the port. Accounts and stats are saved in `data/`.

## Play

Pick a weapon, a color and an armor tier, then choose an FFA, TDM, DOM or BR room (see Last Squad below), open a zombies squad (see Zombies below), or open your own shooting range (see Shooting range below). Bots keep every room at 18 players or more. In TDM and DOM a team short of humans gets one bot for each human it lacks (`BOTS_PER_HUMAN` in `src/server/room.ts`), since people and bots carry the same health. In TDM and DOM every body wears its team color.

Each room rotates through four maps, each 6000 px square: Causeway (a working harbour on two seas: a container ship, a trawler and a patrol boat moored at each quay with walkable decks, gunwales for walls and roofed bridges behind swing doors, a gantry crane over Berth 1, container stacks, a fish shed, customs, a dry dock with a hull on blocks, a salvage yard and a stone causeway across two ponds at the centre, ten districts from Lighthouse Point to the Pier, each with its own lamp colour; the water is an animated toon shader with foam at every shore, caustics, lamp streaks and hull rings, with a canvas fallback; src/client/themes/harbor*.ts), Plaza (city blocks round a wide open square with a fountain at its heart), Old Town (a quarter of small rooms with a doorway in every wall between two long streets, round a planted courtyard) Quarry (long open avenues beside walled pockets, round a monolith of four sandstone blocks), Museum (a night-time heist through a natural history and art museum: four wings, each a district with its name on its doors, from Egypt and the armour hall to modern art, ice age, space and a gift shop, round a marble cross of great halls and a rotunda where two dinosaur skeletons flank zone B), Night Market (a rain-wet lantern-festival night market: two diagonal avenues of striped-awning stalls and food carts cross at a neon crossing, between nine districts from Temple Court to Street Food Row, each with its own paving, light colour, rooftop stories and landmark) and Sub Pen (a covert submarine base: two boats moored in flooded slips under sodium lamps and red alarm beacons, a torpedo store, and eleven districts from Dock 1 to the Control room, each with its own light, floor, landmark and small stories). Park is a city park at dusk with nine named districts (Bandstand Green, Playground, Lantern Pond, Rose Garden, Fountain Plaza, Sports Courts, Boathouse Pond, Picnic Meadow and Orchard), clipped hedges for walls, a fountain ring at the centre and two ponds, each with its own light, floor, set-pieces and signposts (src/client/themes/park*.ts). Wasteland is a settlers' town years after the fall, in nine districts (the Overpass, Gas Station, Shanty Town, Dry Pool, the Crater, Old Church, Bunker, Scrap Market and Crash Site): a collapsed overpass whose half-turn twin is an airliner broken in two, a gas station with its canopy half down and the market that is its twin, a shanty town of scrap shacks and the bunker behind its blast door, a dry pool and an overgrown church, round a radiation crater with a faint green glow, with swing, sliding and double doors, fading roofs, fire barrels, a creaking sign, a scrap wind-pump and a radio that plays to nobody (src/client/themes/wasteland*.ts). Rail Yard is a grand old railway terminus and freight yard at night, the night the last train never left: a vaulted concourse under a stopped clock, a ticket hall with glass sliding doors, waiting rooms and a buffet, and four platforms with a long express standing at them (its middle carriage is a corridor you can walk through on sliding doors), with the freight yard (coal hopper, boxcar sidings, signal box, water tower, turntable, engine shed, goods office, a rusty crane, a tunnel mouth and a level crossing) in the north east, and the half-turn twin of every part, dressed as a different place (goods hall, mail train, coaling tower, overgrown turntable, sealed tunnel, carriage shed), in the south and west. Locomotive and carriages are polygon walls, doors slide, swing and double, platform canopies and rooms have fading roofs, and the clock tower, water tower and crane are landmarks seen from across the map (src/client/themes/railyard*.ts). Embassy is a gala interrupted: a modern Office Wing and the old Residence (its half-turn twin, in walnut, damask and warm lamps) round a glass-domed Atrium with the republic's emblem in marble, with a Lobby and Security Checkpoint behind automatic glass sliders and a Kitchen for its twin, a Front Lawn with a fountain and an armoured motorcade (the Service Lot and its caterers' vans on the other half), a topiary garden and a hedge maze, and fifteen rooms and galleries behind swing, sliding and double doors with fading roofs: the Ambassador's Suite with its huge desk, globe and sleeping cat, a conference room with a long oval table and a helipad on its roof, a Server Room with blinking racks, an Archives vault, a Ballroom laid for the gala, a grand staircase hall of curved walls and a portrait gallery with a toy soldier in a gilt frame (src/client/themes/embassy*.ts, src/shared/maps/embassy.ts). Airbase is Kestrel Field at night, three minutes into a scramble: two C-130s half loaded in huge roofed hangars (polygon walls with real fuselage and wings, KILROY on one fin and a rubber duck on its cargo ramp, a pilot's helmet left on a wing), a fighter on jacks in the maintenance bay, a round control tower with a lit glass cab and a sweeping beacon round zone B, a helicopter whose rotor turns over its pad, a fuel farm of round tanks, a radar dome and comms hut, barracks with corridors and bunk rooms, a mess hall with double swing doors, a parade ground and an armoury, with sliding hangar doors, swing and double swing doors, fading roofs, taxiway lights that chase toward the tower, a windsock and a flag, and crows, pigeons, a cat, moths and steam for company, in nine districts each with its own floor, light and landmarks (src/client/themes/airbase*.ts). Summit is a ski resort the night a blizzard closed the mountain: a timber Summit Lodge (a fireplace great hall with a bearskin rug round zone A, a bar behind swing saloon doors, a rental shop behind a roll-up shutter, guest-room corridors of swing doors with connecting rooms) and the Alpine Spa that is its half-turn twin, a Hot Tub Deck with steaming tubs and a barrel sauna (the Sauna Yard and its cold plunges in the turn), a chairlift station with the chain across its gate and a LIFTS CLOSED sign (the Ice Garden in the turn), chairs sliding along the cable overhead, a frozen lake with a hockey rink round zone B, open pools, a fishing hut (a Zamboni shed in the turn), a Snowcat Garage with the groomer in its bay (the Cable Car Terminal in the turn), a fuel house and, in the turn, the Observatory Ridge with its dome, snow-laden pine woods round a hunter's cabin with an elder pine strung with lights (the Tree Farm in the turn), abandoned skis, a half-built snowman, a yeti trail that ends at a wall, a penguin at the pool, ravens, foxes and falling snow (src/client/themes/summit*.ts, src/shared/maps/summit*.ts). Every mode loads the next map when a round restarts. TDM is won at 150 team kills or, after 12 minutes, by the team ahead on score and then on kills. DOM is won at 3000 points or, after 15 minutes, by the team ahead. A DOM point is taken by standing on it with no enemy on it: a lone soldier takes a free point in 3 seconds, and each teammate beside them adds half that rate again, up to 2.5 times it (1, 1.5, 2, then 2.5 times for four or more; `src/shared/zonerate.ts`). An enemy's point must first be turned neutral at the same rate, a partial enemy capture drains at it before yours starts, an empty point lets a stranded capture bleed off at the lone rate, and a point with both teams on it is contested and holds still. A held point scores 5 points a second. Each point stands a flag pole at its centre: the flag climbs in the taker's colour as a capture fills and comes down as a held point is turned neutral, flies at the top of a held point, and hangs still while it is contested, when hazard stripes of both colours flash round its ring. A disc of the taker's colour grows from the centre with the capture and shrinks as it drains, chevrons run round the ring one per soldier working it (with a ×1.5 to ×2.5 badge by the flag), each point lights the ground round it in its colour so it reads at night, and a change of hands throws a ring pulse, confetti and a puff of smoke (`src/client/zoneart.ts`). The objective strip's A, B and C pins fill like a clock in the taker's colour, carry a small flag at the world flag's height and a pip per soldier on the point, and flash both colours when contested; the minimap fills each point the same way, and an edge arrow points to an off-screen point your team is taking or one of yours being taken. You hear a tick rising in pitch through a capture you stand in, a bugle when your team takes a point, the same falling when it loses one, and a buzz when a point is contested. An FFA round runs 10 minutes and goes to the player with the most kills, bot or human, fewest deaths breaking a tie; a human who reaches 30 kills ends it early and leads the podium. A timed round that nobody wins, such as an FFA round with no kills or a dead-even TDM round, restarts at once on the next map. The objective line and the FFA leaderboard count the round's time down. The next map is announced 15 seconds ahead.

The world wears the same kit as the interface, so nothing looks borrowed from another art set: every solid, body and gun is a flat shape in one muted military palette with an ink outline and two hard cel-shading steps (a light edge toward the light, a dark one away from it), shadows are crisp drop shadows, the floor is calm bone concrete in big slabs, concrete blocks are riveted gunmetal plates, sandstone is khaki plate, planters and crates are olive, the arena is framed in the interface's orange hazard tape, and the horde wears sickly versions of the same olive, khaki, steel and rust. The interface wears one field kit throughout, menu and HUD alike: matte gunmetal plates with clipped corners and an orange corner bracket, bone-white Barlow Condensed type (self-hosted in `public/fonts`, under the SIL Open Font License), and a single signal orange for whatever you can act on or have chosen. Guns are stylized rather than realistic (`src/client/gunart.ts`): each class is a few chunky, exaggerated shapes in a bold ink outline with two cel steps, a stubby pistol, a boxy SMG with a long mag, a fat-barrelled shotgun with a wooden pump, a rifle with a red-dot and a curved mag, a long bolt-action under a big scope and an LMG with a drum box, so each reads at a glance. The loadout, evolve picks and kill feed show them side on; in a player's hands and on the ground beside a corpse the same parts are drawn top-down, like everything else in the world. Every evolution reshapes its class's art by its look: a longer barrel, extra barrels (stacked side on, side by side from above), a pistol in each hand, an accent stripe. Each class gun's card in the loadout shows power, rate, reach and mobility as bars against the other classes, callouts land as stamped plates, and the death card is edged in hazard tape.

On a phone the game plays on its side, and a phone held upright is asked to turn sideways. Where the browser can go fullscreen (Android Chrome, iPad Safari), Play and then the first tap in each match ask for fullscreen and a landscape lock (`src/client/fullscreen.ts`); a player who leaves fullscreen mid-match is not pulled back until the next one. An iPhone has no page fullscreen, so its menu opens with a tip to add Tinwar to the home screen, which then runs fullscreen on its side under its own icon (`manifest.webmanifest`, the `apple-mobile-web-app-*` tags, `viewport-fit=cover`). The left thumb walks and the right thumb aims and fires, each wherever it lands on its half of the screen; faint rings show where, labelled Move and Aim · Fire for the first five seconds (on a phone, only in your first match). A phone on its side (a touch screen under 520 px tall) gets its own condensed HUD (`src/client/phonelayout.ts`, one pure layout shared by the canvas HUD, the DOM buttons and its test) that keeps the middle of the screen clear and never stacks one element on another, inside the notch and home-bar insets. Screen space is precious there, so it shows only what matters and keeps the rest until it does or you ask (`src/client/phonefocus.ts`, pinned by its test). Always on: a small health cross with its armor top left, the rounds left on the reload button and the ability on its button in a column at the right edge, the two sticks, the cog, a small minimap under the cross, and one slim status line top centre only where the mode needs one (Domination's scores round its zones, the zombies night with what is left of it, the core and the survivors, and Last Squad's ring and squads). On demand: your level, a pin for each perk picked this life, and the score where no scoreboard chip shows it, beside the cross for a moment after they change (or a tap on the cross); the minimap opens big for a few seconds on a tap, with pings drawn on it either way; the chat folds to a small pip with a count while there is something to read, and a tap shows the lines; the kill feed under the top-right row keeps only your own kills, deaths and assists and the big events (a bounty, a long streak ended, someone hunted, a squad out, an airdrop, a squadmate down), for three and a half seconds; the clock comes up for an FFA or TDM round's final minute and TDM's score for a moment after it moves; the emote (GG) button only in a round's last half minute or once it is over; the scoreboard chip with your place and score (a tap opens it for three seconds); the round's objective spelled out once per mode on that device, and callouts (Night 1, a streak) for two seconds under the status line; medals as a small chip beside the minimap; the range's readout folded to its last hit until tapped; and the radio's prompt only the first time (its Radio button stays). Hints, the revive prompt and the level-up pill sit low between the sticks. Speech bubbles shrink there to one at a time, gone sooner, and the squad's invite link waits in the pause menu. The buttons are icons rather than letters: reload is a circular arrow that fills round as the reload runs and glows red on an empty mag, and the ability button shows the ability's own icon with a cooldown sweep and the seconds left, gold when ready; until an ability is picked the button is not shown at all. Touch screens get larger HUD text, and the HUD shrinks no further than 90% on a small screen. On big screens the whole interface grows with the window (`src/client/uiscale.ts`): from a 900 px short side it scales up, 1.6 times at 2560×1440 and 2.4 times at 4K, so the HUD, the menu and every overlay take the same share of the screen at any resolution, drawn sharp at the screen's pixel density. Text over the world (forecasts, hints, name tags) sits on the kit's dark plates so it reads on the light floor. The level-up dock opens collapsed to a pulsing pill so it never covers the fight until tapped. Touch aim has a light assist: when an enemy's lead point, found the same way the bots lead, lies within 7 degrees of the thumb's aim, the aim turns toward it by at most 2.5 degrees. `node scripts/aim-assist-bench.ts` measures it at about 20% more hits for a thumb that leads poorly, almost all of it at range.

A round leaves the muzzle at five times its cruise speed and eases down over its first couple of hundred px, so up close it lands before anyone can step aside, even a target already strafing, while at range most rounds fly slowly enough to see coming and sidestep; sniper rounds cruise fast but get a gentler kick out of the muzzle, so a sniper does not also own close range. How a gun handles comes from what it is (see Handling below): pistols and SMGs fire as true on the run as standing still, a long, heavy gun sways on the move (an assault rifle about 1.7 times its still spread, a machine gun about 2 times, a shotgun a little), and a planted sniper is pinpoint down to a small floor (see Weapon roles below). No gun is ever a laser: each has a spread floor it never fires under, however planted, steady or perked (bigger rounds and shorter barrels group worse: about 1.3 degrees either side of the aim for a pistol, 0.7 for an SMG, 0.6 for the long guns; a set-down bipod's own is 0.7), so the reticle never closes to a point. Every gun blooms when fired fast: from the second round of a spray (a pistol's sixth, a burst gun's round after its burst, the semi-auto snipers' and shotguns' third) each round kicks the spread wider by what its calibre kicks against the gun's weight and length, the first kicks hardest and each later one less as the cone nears its cap (see The bloom curve below), and it cools once you pause, so paced taps stay tight. On the move a gun blooms the full way; standing it grows only part as much (a light gun more, a heavy one less: 70% for a pistol, about half for a rifle, 40% for an LMG) and with a bipod set down 30%, always on top of its floor. Every mode handles guns the same way: bloom, moving spread, suppression, muzzle kick and recoil apply in Zombies and Last Squad just as in FFA, TDM and DOM. Each round is drawn as a lit slug with a hot white-yellow core, an amber body and a warm tracer, fatter and with a longer streak the harder the gun hits, glowing at night. A round that lands knocks its victim back along the shot, in proportion to the damage and the gun's class: about 15 px/s from a pistol round, 5 from an SMG's, a point-blank shotgun blast the 120 px/s cap, a bolt-action round about 95, and blasts up to 180 px/s. The shove bleeds off in about a quarter second, slides through cover like any step so nobody is pushed into a wall, and heavy armor lets only 70% of it through; zombies take it by weight (runners most, brutes and the colossus not at all). The server owns it and sends your own shove in your snapshot so your movement prediction replays it, and a victim visibly flinches, flashes and sprays more the bigger the hit. Muzzle flashes grow with the gun, with a brief floor light pool and, for shotguns and snipers, a big bloom. Bots play by the same rules: they aim where the round will meet a moving target, found from the target's velocity and the round's flight along the muzzle curve, with a slightly different judgment of the lead each time they engage, their aim widens when suppressed, and rounds cracking past make them sidestep and duck like a hit would. An enemy round passing within 60 px of you suppresses you: your spread widens by up to 25%, a planted sniper loses its pinpoint aim, the edges of your screen close in under a heavy dark vignette that hides them almost entirely at full suppression, and the HUD switches to its high-contrast colors so you can still read your ammo. Each round adds what its gun suppresses, and suppression holds only 0.15 seconds before it fades, so only sustained automatic fire keeps it building: an LMG pins you down in under a second and its shadow lingers for about a second and a half after it stops, an assault rifle or SMG does so in one to two seconds, while a pistol barely builds any and a sniper's crack is shaken off in about half a second. What you carry slows you down: every kilogram of gun and armor (and a little for a long gun) takes the same share off your walk and more off your sprint (light, medium and heavy armor weigh 3, 6 and 9 kg, about 10%, 19% and 29% of your speed; a machine gun costs about a quarter), but no loadout drops below 58% of base speed; the Lightweight attachment gives 25% back. A grenade, mine or other blast shakes your screen, harder the closer and bigger it is, and big guns (shotguns, rifles, hand cannons, machine guns) kick the camera back along your aim when you fire. A fallen player lies where they died for 30 seconds, a grey body crossed out with an X, knocked back along the killing shot, in a pool of blood sprayed out the far side, with their gun slid to a random spot and angle beside them; an explosive death leaves a darker, scorched body on soot with the gun flung further. Corpses fade out over the last 3 seconds, and a new map clears the field. In Zombies every zombie the squad kills stays where it fell until the night ends, eyes crossed out and limbs splayed in a pool of ichor sprayed away from whoever killed it; a pile of them merges into one outlined heap, and the whole field fades at dawn.

**Health.** A person and a bot carry the same health and regen, and every gun and body stat is the same for both (speed, spread, bloom, reload, cooldowns, pickups, perks: `test/bot-parity.test.ts` runs a bot and a person through the same inputs and checks the sim treats them alike). The one rule between them: a bot's damage to a person counts 0.75 (`BOT_DAMAGE_TO_HUMAN` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, on every path: rounds, blasts, the knife, gas, fire), so a room of bots can't simply grind you down. Person on person, person on bot and bot on bot all hit for the gun's own damage. Armor blocks a flat share of each hit: 8%, 16% or 24%.

Points in one life raise your level. A kill pays 100, and with its medals and the catch-up bonus a typical kill comes to about 165 points (measured in rooms of bots; `test/balance.test.ts` holds it). At 220 points, around your first or second kill, you pick an attachment. At 450, around the second or third kill, your gun evolves, and at 1100, around the sixth or seventh, it evolves again, each time into one of two branches: 6 class guns, 12 stage-1 guns and 24 stage-2 guns. Between the two evolutions, at 700 and 900 points (around kills four and five), you pick a tier-2 perk (four drawn at random for each life from fifteen) and an ability, so a pick comes about every kill. On a desktop the top left shows what the life has picked (`src/client/loadout.ts`): one plain line with your level and what comes next ("LV 2 · Next: Perk · 520 / 700", "Pick a perk" while one waits, or the score once the ladder is done), and under it a tile for each pick: the evolved gun, the attachment, the perk and the ability, each with its icon and short name. Nothing shows for a pick not yet earned; each tile stamps in as its pick lands, hovering one with the cursor says what it does, and the ability's tile sweeps through its cooldown with the seconds left and turns orange with a glow and its key when it is ready. A gun evolve is always offered before any perk you have not picked yet, so an ignored perk never holds back a new gun. Dying resets your score, perks and gun to the class gun. While your level is below the average level of the other living players, every point you earn counts 1.5 times. A player holding a stage-2 gun is hunted: every enemy minimap pings their position every 2.5 seconds and whenever they fire an unsilenced shot (not with Ninja: then only the 2.5-second ping finds them), the kill feed announces it, and killing them pays a 150-point bounty on top of the kill. An attacker who dealt at least 30% of a victim's max health earns a 50-point assist when someone else gets the kill. A player killed by their own blast gives the kill to whoever hurt them most in the last 10 seconds.

**Weapon roles.** Every class has one job in the fight, and at each evolve the two branches are sidegrades that change how you play, not just how hard you hit. The evolve pick shows each gun's role in a line and an icon for each thing it changes (`src/shared/roles.ts`; hover a tile for what each icon means). `node scripts/gun-audit.ts [--moving] [--roles]` prints every gun's numbers with its damage per second and seconds to kill per armor at 150, 400, 700 and 1000 px.

**Sniper tempo.** A sniper is a commitment, not a spam gun. The bolt-actions (Bolt-action 1.75 s between shots, Longshot and Piercer 1.92 s, Artillery 1.7 s) work the bolt by hand after every round: the soldier's support hand leaves the fore-end, lifts the handle, draws it back, runs it home and locks it over exactly the fire interval (`boltScene` in `src/client/reloadanim.ts`, on the `BOLT` beats in `src/client/reloadbeats.ts` that the bolt foley also plays on), and your reticle stays grey, faint and dotless at its true gap until the next round is chambered, then flashes bright; a click while the bolt is still well short of home plays the "not yet" tick and shakes it. Each round also kicks the cone open, even planted: a pinpoint gun's bloom is a kick of its own still spread on top of its floor, so straight after a shot a bolt-action's cone opens to about 3.4 degrees either side of the aim (6.5 times its floor), and it takes about 2.4 s to close back to the floor. The fastest follow-up, ready at 1.75 s, still flies about 4.5 times as wide as a patient one (about 2.4 degrees), and firing every round as fast as the bolt allows holds it at the widest; you see it on the reticle, which opens with each shot and visibly tightens back. The semi-autos (Semi-auto Rifle and Ghost 0.39 s, Repeater 0.25 s) kick less and settle sooner: two quick rounds fly clean, each further quick one widens the cone (about 1.8 then 3 degrees for the Semi-auto), and it is gone about 1.2 s after the last. One shot from full health drops: the Bolt-action, only the unarmored (two rounds drop any armor); the Longshot and Artillery, up to light armor; the Piercer, up to medium; nothing drops heavy armor in one except an Artillery shell with the Demolitions perk; the semi-autos always take two or more. Bots fire their snipers the same way: a bot lets the bloom settle before its next long shot (`tapRhythm` reads the gun's bloom).

What the rules add beyond the old stat sheet (`GunRules` in `src/shared/defs.ts`): `falloff` (a round keeps full damage out to `startPx`, then fades to `minMul` by `endPx`: SMGs, shotguns, the Machine Pistol, Akimbo, Gunslinger), `deploy` (stand still for `ms` and the spread is multiplied by `spreadMul`: the bipod LMGs), `shoveMul` (knockback per hit) and `breach` (a round that strikes a swing door blows it open). The rest of a gun's handling is not written per gun but worked out from its build (see Handling): its sway on the move (`movingSpreadAdd`), its spread floor (`minSpread`; a bipod's `deploy.minSpread` replaces it while set down), its bloom's kick, cap, recovery and standing share (`bloom.still`; `deploy.bloom` with a bipod down), its post-sprint `settle`, and how fast it lets you walk and sprint. Bots fight each gun from the distance its job wants (`GUN_BAND` in `src/server/bot/intent.ts`): rushers close in and never back off, a plant-to-aim or bipod gun holds its lane, and from 280 px out a bot whose gun blooms with its rounds (an assault rifle, a sniper, an SMG or auto pistol that holds a lane) fires in taps of its gun's `tap` rounds at most, holding each next round until its cone at that range fits the target about as well as at rest, while rushers and machine guns hose, a machine gun because a stream pins (`tapRhythm`).

**Handling.** Every gun and evolution has a weight, a length and a calibre (`kg`, `cm`, `calibre` in `src/shared/defs.ts`; each calibre's muzzle energy is in `CALIBRES`), and every armor tier a weight; a few documented formulas in `src/shared/handling.ts` work out the rest, so a gun handles like what it is instead of from a hand-set number. A bigger round kicks harder and groups a hair worse; a heavier gun soaks more of the kick but sways more on the move and takes longer to bring back on target; a longer gun is steadier and groups tighter but swings more (its moment of inertia, weight times length squared, sets the walking sway and the post-sprint settle). Each round's bloom kick is its energy (both rounds of a paired pull) over the gun's weight and length, grown by the square root of its cadence past an assault rifle's 9 rounds a second (faster than that, the muzzle is never back on target before the next round), up to a cap from the round and the weight; `cap / kick` is how many rounds a held trigger takes to reach the cap. A light, short gun is held close and its muzzle cannot wander far, so its whole bloom (kick and cap alike, so it takes the same rounds to its cap) is held to its reach, `0.6 + 0.4 x I^2 / (I^2 + 1.5^2)` of the build's, I being its moment of inertia (`HANDLING.capReach`): a pistol 0.6, an SMG 0.67, a Skirmisher 0.62, an assault rifle 0.94, a machine gun about 1, so spraying an LMG stays punished. A shotgun's bloom is held to 0.65 of its build at most (`HANDLING.shotgunCapMul`): its pellet pattern is its spread. Full auto is punished standing as well as moving: standing grows 0.6 (the heaviest MG) to 0.95 (a pistol or SMG) of the moving bloom, 0.85 for an assault rifle, so planting your feet helps only a little.

**The bloom curve.** Shooters model spray bloom a few ways. Battlefield adds a fixed spread increase per shot (SIPS) up to a max spread and takes it back at a fixed rate a second: a straight line that hits its cap like a wall. Counter-Strike adds a fixed inaccuracy a shot and decays it exponentially (a recovery time to a tenth), which under held fire is a leaky integrator that creeps toward a plateau set by the fire rate, and whose tail never quite clears between taps. Valorant gives the first shot full accuracy and ramps error over the next few rounds to a plateau. The usual diminishing-returns forms are the exponential approach (`b += (cap - b) k`: never reaches the cap, one fixed shape), tanh (`cap tanh(n / n0)`: the same, asymptotic) and a per-shot kick scaled by how much room is left, `kick (1 - b/cap)^p`. Tinwar uses the last, with 0 < p < 1, because its exact solution is a closed form in the rounds fired:

`share(n) = 1 - (1 - n/N)^q` for n < N, and 1 from N on, with q = 1/(1 - p),

where n is the rounds of the spray past its first (`free`: 1 for a full-auto gun, a burst gun's whole burst and one more (so a held burst gun stays on its cone between bursts), 2 for the semi-auto rifles and shotguns, 3 for the Marksman, 5 for a pistol so a sidearm clicked as fast as it cycles stays on its cone), N = `cap / kick` is the rounds to the cap and q is the curve's `shape`. The round after the free ones adds q times the mean kick and each later one less, as `(1 - b/cap)^(1 - 1/q)`, so a held trigger bites at once and slows as it nears the cap, landing on it at N rounds with zero slope and never past it. Unlike the asymptotic curves it keeps N meaningful (it is still the rounds a spray takes to cap), q = 1 is exactly the old straight line, and a large q tends to the exponential approach, so one shape knob spans them. The shape comes from the gun's weight, `q = clamp(2 / (kg / 3.6)^0.45, 1.3, 3.4)` (`HANDLING.shape`): a light gun (a pistol 3.4, an SMG 2.4) snaps up and flattens, a heavy one (an LMG 1.3, the Minigun 1.3) rises slower but carries its momentum nearly straight to the cap, the assault rifle sits at 2. The sim keeps one number, the spray's heat in rounds; `bloomShare` and `coolSpray` in `src/shared/sim/trigger.ts` are the only implementation, used by the sim, the page's prediction (`src/client/fire.ts`) and a bot's read of its own cone (`ownBloom` in `src/server/bot/motor.ts`). Recovery is linear in that heat once no round has left for `settleMs`, so the cone comes back down the curve it climbed: slowly off the cap, then quickly near rest. A full-auto class's `settleMs` sits just past the longest gap between held rounds of any of its guns (105 ms for the SMGs, 140 for the assault rifles and the machine guns), so a held trigger never cools between rounds and a let-go one starts cooling at once. A full spray's heat then clears in the gun's `recoverMs`, `123 x (kg / 3.6)^0.6 x (cm / 90)^0.7` (`HANDLING.recover`; length weighs more than weight, as in the gun's moment of inertia): about 60 ms for an SMG, 40 for a Skirmisher, 120 for an assault rifle, 245 for an LMG and 320 for the Minigun, so a small gun is brought back twice as fast as under the old `190 x (kg / 3.6)^0.5 x (cm / 90)^0.3` and a big one about 1.3 times. A scoped gun keeps that old formula times 6.7 (`HANDLING.scopeRecover`, then 1.6 more for a bolt-action), so a sniper's bloom still takes its seconds to settle. The reticle (and the cone a shot gets) eases bloom back out at the gun's own pace too: over its `recoverMs`, kept between 100 and 250 ms (`spreadEaseDownMs`; see Sprint below), so a capped spray is back within a tenth of its rest in about 0.23 s for an SMG, 0.2 s for a Skirmisher, 0.3 s for an assault rifle, 0.57 s for the Minigun and 0.6 s for an LMG (it was 0.37, 0.37, 0.43, 0.57 and 0.6 while every gun's bloom eased out over the full 250 ms): an SMG's reticle settles in under 0.4 of an LMG's time. That is what makes bursting pay at range: standing at 450 px, four rounds and a 150 ms let-go land about 7.4 hits a second from an assault rifle against 5.5 held, and 6.3 from an LMG against 6.3 held, and up close (150 px) a held trigger wins with every gun. An SMG is not made to burst at 450 px: it is past its falloff there (its rounds do 30%), its cone at rest (6.3 degrees either side) is twice a body's width, and its bloom is now small and gone so soon that a held trigger loses little (5.1 hits a second held, 5.0 from four rounds and a quick 100 ms let-go). Its rest cone is left as it is: tightening it would make it a mid-range gun, which is the assault rifle's job. A short tap sits low on the curve where it is steep and clears in a blink (a tap every half second is back on the first round's cone every time, four a second stays within 15 per cent, the kick easing out over `SPREAD_EASE`), while a long spray has banked heat on the plateau and holds its width a while; the Counter-Strike way round (fast first, slow tail) would leave a residue on every tap and reward spraying. An assault rifle held down is 1.6 times as wide by round 3, 3.1 times by round 10 and caps at 3.9 near round 19; an SMG 1.2, 1.5 and 1.6 (its cap a third lower than its build alone would give: 3.8 degrees of bloom, from 5.6); an LMG 1.2, 1.8 and 2.4 by round 30. Burst guns fly each burst on its first round's cone and cool between bursts. `tap` (written per class: three for an assault rifle, four for an SMG or auto pistol) is how many rounds a disciplined tap fires: a bot's tap rhythm and the Disciplined medal. A set-down bipod is the one thing that tames a held machine gun: it still blooms, 1.4 to 1.8 times by round 30, against 1.9 to 2.8 times stood up without it. A scoped gun's kick, cap and decay are scaled by one shared factor (the sight picture is lost and found, not just the muzzle moved) and a bolt-action's further by another (working the bolt breaks the cheek weld): that is where a sniper's big per-shot bloom comes from. Walk speed falls by the same share for every kilogram carried (gun, plus a little for its length, plus armor), never under the 58% floor, and the share of the sprint bonus you keep falls with the square of the load. What stays written per gun is what its role needs and physics does not give: bursts, the bipod, the rev-up, falloff, the shotgun's pellet pattern and the gun's base spread, plus explicit, marked `overrides` on a gun (none are needed today) and two class exceptions, both the shotgun's: every shotgun settles off a sprint in 0.6 of what its build gives (`HANDLING.shotgunSettleMul`; a pump gun about 1.3 s, not 2.1), because coming off a sprint and blasting is its job, and its bloom is held to 0.65 of its build (`HANDLING.shotgunCapMul`), because its pellet pattern is its spread. **View.** With no bonus you see 700 px to either side (`WORLD.viewRadius`, zoomed in about 10% from 780 so bodies and rounds read bigger; the height follows the screen's shape, so 16:9 sees 700 by 394 px each way from you, and the stronger aim look-ahead gives back the ground down the aim). Three things widen it: the gun's scope (`viewMul`: +20 to +36% on every sniper, the Scout +12%, the Marksman +7%, the Executioner, Rail Slug, Battle Rifle and Grenadier +5%), Optics (+12%, an attachment for pistols and assault rifles) and Recon (+8%). They stack with diminishing returns up to a cap for the class (`VIEW` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, `viewMulFor` in `src/shared/sim/stats.ts`): each bonus closes its share of the gap still left to the cap, so one alone is worth all of itself, the next less, and the total never reaches 1.45 times the base for a sniper or 1.18 for any other class. The widest view in play is a Piercer with Recon at 1.38 times (963 px); a Scout with Optics and Recon sees 1.17 times (818 px). The camera also leans toward your aim (below). The camera, the server's snapshot culling (with its aim look-ahead widening) and bot sight all use that one view radius, so a bot sees no further than a human with the same kit. Bots see by what a person's screen shows (`botSight` in `src/server/bot/awareness.ts`): the typical 16:9 screen (`BOT_VIEW_ASPECT` in `src/shared/protocol.ts`), 700 by 394 px each way with no bonus, widened toward where the bot aims by the full lean a person's camera gets (`lookReach`, below), with the same scope, Optics and Recon bonuses and caps. So a scoped sniper bot sees as far down its aim as a scoped person (about 1520 px for a plain Bolt-action, 1660 for a Piercer with Optics and Recon) but only about 500 to 550 px up and down beside it and its view radius behind it, and has to turn to see the rest; it used to see its whole view radius every way (896 to 977 px). Its snapshot is the interest a person with its gun, perks and aim is sent (`botSnapshot` in `src/server/bot/tick.ts`), and a test holds the two boxes equal, so a bot spots you when your screen could have spotted it.

**Aim look-ahead.** The camera leans toward where you aim, further the nearer the cursor is to the screen's edge (full at 80% of the way there, in any direction): by 36% of your view radius for a shotgun, 39% for an SMG, 45% for a pistol, 48% for an LMG, 50% for an assault rifle and 70% for a sniper (`LOOK_AHEAD.share`), so aimed at the side edge of a 16:9 screen an assault rifle shows 350 px more ground that way (1050 px down the aim, about what the wider view showed before the zoom) and a scoped sniper about 630 (1520 px). A gun that reaches past the edge of its view (every sniper, the long rifles, slugs and machine guns) leans at least far enough that its full lean shows its whole range, up to 75% of the view (`lookReach` in `src/shared/lookahead.ts`). However far it leans, your own soldier stays at least 15% of the screen from every edge, on any shape of screen (an ellipse at 70% of what is on screen, `boundLean` in `src/client/camera.ts`, wide enough that a scoped sniper leans in full down the long side of a 16:9 screen), so a lean toward a short side (up and down on a wide screen, sideways on a phone held upright) stops sooner. A thumb on the aim stick leans 70% as far and keeps the soldier a quarter of the screen from the edges. The lean eases in and out (an exponential follow, a little quicker while firing or planted) and snaps only on a respawn or teleport. Settings, Aim look-ahead: Normal, Low (0.3 of it, about the first, subtler lean) or Off; reduced motion halves it.

**Minimap and hearing.** The minimap shows your teammates, the objectives, the supply plane and drops, and an enemy only when something marks them: a hunted player's ping (see above) or a Tracker mark. Firing does not put anyone on it, silenced or not. You hear gunfire instead: another soldier's shot plays in stereo where it was fired, fading out at 1.2 times your view radius, and a silenced one is a muffled thup. Bots have no stereo, so the server gives each one a hearing cue (`BOT_HEARING` in `src/shared/sim/hearing.ts`, carried in a bot's own snapshot as `heard`, never on the wire): an enemy's unsilenced shot within 2.4 times the base view radius (1680 px), twice a person's earshot on purpose to keep bots competitive (a sniper or LMG carries 25% further, 2100 px, a shotgun 10%), a silenced shot only within 350 px, each placed only roughly, off by up to 20% of its distance. Earshot has the screen's shape (`earDist`): those reaches are to either side, and only 1/1.78 of them above or below (945 px for an ordinary shot), so a bot hears no further up or down than a person's 16:9 screen shows against its width; the enemies it only remembers when it counts those about it to weigh a fight (`othersOn`, 750 px to either side, 422 up or down) are measured the same way. A bot's own scope, Optics and Recon widen its eyes, not its ears (a fully scoped sniper bot used to hear 2345 px, 2931 for a loud gun, half the map), and a shot it placed only thanks to the loud bonus, past 1680 px, is somewhere to go and look, never a spot it points its gun at (`aimsAtLead`). A bot with nothing in sight goes to search toward what it heard, the nearest hunted ping or Tracker mark first; a flashed bot hears nothing.

The tier 3 abilities include two that deal no damage. **Flashbang** (9 s cooldown) is thrown like a grenade with a 0.7 s fuse. When it bursts, everyone within 360 px with a clear line to it (walls block) is flashed for up to 3 seconds, scaled down with distance (to 20% at the edge) and by where they look (looking at it is full, facing away is 35%); your own team and you are caught too. A flashed human's screen whites out, then fades with an afterimage of the frame the flash hit, while the mix muffles and a tinnitus ring plays. A flashed bot is blind on the same server-side flash state: it takes in no new sightings, minimap marks or gunfire cues while the flash is strong (it keeps only its memory of where enemies were), then its reaction is up to 0.7 s slower and its aim error up to four times wider as the flash fades; it sprays at the last known spot, backs off or stands still. A bot that sees a flashbang in the air notices it 60% of the time (after 0.25 s), and turns its back and holds fire. **Smoke** (12 s cooldown) bursts after 0.8 s into a 180 px cloud that blooms, drifts slowly and lasts 10 seconds, thinning over the last 2.5. Bullets pass through it but sight does not: the server leaves out of a player's snapshot any enemy whose line from them crosses a cloud (in it or behind it), so nothing on the wire sees through it, and bots, who think from that same snapshot, cannot either. Inside a cloud you see only 110 px around you; teammates stay visible, and gunfire can still be heard. A bot that loses its target in smoke holds position instead of pushing in. Bots flash a far enemy (380 to 480 px, or the corner one just went behind) so the burst lands clear of themselves, and smoke when they are hurt or under fire.

**Sprint.** Hold Shift (on a phone, push the move stick out to the outer ring that appears beyond its walking ring) while moving to sprint: you move up to 1.65 times faster, applied after the 58% loadout floor so Lightweight stacks with it, and how much of that bonus you get depends on your load (`sprintShareOf`: a light load gets a little more than all of it, an assault rifle about all, an LMG about 60%, a Minigun, a Juggernaut or anything in heavy armor hardly any). A pistol sprints at about 455 px/s and crosses a 6000 px map edge to edge in about 13 s, against 23 s walking; the heaviest loadout sprints at about 158 px/s. The gun is lowered while you sprint: it cannot fire, though you can still reload and use abilities. A click ends the sprint (the held key no longer counts) and fires on the same tick: there is no wait for the gun. What a sprint costs is **bloom**. The moment it ends your spread is about 4 times the gun's moving spread (`SPRINT.settleMul`, wider than any walk) and it eases out (a squared curve, so most of it is gone by the halfway point) to whatever your stance gives (your walking spread if you keep moving, your standing or planted spread if you stop) over the gun's settle time (`settleRulesOf`; Marathon halves it, Steady Hands takes a quarter off). Settling fast off a sprint is a light gun's edge, and it comes from the gun's weight and length (`settleMs` in Handling): about 0.3 s for a pistol, a Gunslinger or a Skirmisher, 0.5 s for an SMG, 1.4 s for a Carbine, 1.7 s for a Heavy SMG, 2 s for an assault rifle or a shotgun, and 2.5 s for a sniper, an LMG and the heaviest guns. It breaks a planted sniper's pinpoint while it lasts; suppression and a shot's own bloom stack on top. **Spread never jumps**: every change of it (walking to standing, a bipod setting down, a sprint starting, suppression, a perk, a gun swap) eases in over at least 250 ms (`SPREAD_EASE`; the sim keeps the last nine ticks' spread targets and a shot gets their mean, `easeSpread`), and only a shot's own bloom kick lands at once. Bloom comes back down at the gun's own pace, never at once: the sim eases the spread without bloom and the bloom on top separately, and once the bloom is falling it ramps out over the gun's `recoverMs` kept between 100 and 250 ms (`spreadEaseDownMs`; 100 ms for an SMG or a pistol, about 125 for an assault rifle, the full 250 for an LMG, a Minigun or a sniper), while any rise, and any change of the spread under the bloom, still takes the full 250 ms. Your reticle draws that same eased spread: the client steps it tick by tick from your inputs exactly as the server does (`spreadOf` in `src/client/fire.ts`), so it opens wide while you sprint, is wide the moment you stop and visibly tightens as the bloom settles. The sprint state and the post-sprint bloom ride in your own snapshot (`sprint`, `sprintSpeed`, `settle`, `settleMs`; `settle` is the share of the bloom still to ease out) so prediction of your movement, your shots and your reticle match the server. You and others see a sprinting soldier with the gun carried across the chest, a longer stride, the head leaning in, bigger dust puffs and faint speed streaks; when the sprint ends the gun swings back up to the aim in a short, purely cosmetic swing (`swingMsOf`, from the gun's build: 0.15 s for a pistol up to 0.28 s for the heaviest), overshooting a hair as it locks on. Bots sprint only to travel (after 1.5 s out of any fight) with no enemy in sight or to run to cover; they walk the moment they see one, so they can fire, and a tapping bot lets the post-sprint bloom settle before a long shot as it does its own spray bloom. Shift+M still toggles the music.

The arena itself has surprises in Free for All, Team Deathmatch and Domination (`src/shared/sim/barrels.ts`, `src/shared/sim/airdrop.ts`, drawn by `src/client/arenafx.ts`):

- **Explosive barrels.** Rust-red barrels stand in clusters of two or three on every versus map (a `b` in the map grid; they turn about the centre like crates, and `node scripts/map-lint.ts` fails a barrel in a wall, on a crate, over a zone, within 120 px of a spawn region or without its half-turn twin). Bullets stop on them and wear them down; a hurt barrel leaks smoke and sparks, and at zero it hisses and flashes for under half a second, then bursts as a blast of 170 px. A barrel caught in a blast lights a shorter fuse the nearer it was, so a row goes off one after another. A barrel kill credits whoever lit the barrel (teammates are spared in team modes, and your own barrel still hurts you) and earns **Kaboom**; a chain of two or more barrels that kills two or more players earns its igniter **Chain Reaction**. A burst barrel leaves a scorch and stands again after 45 seconds, though never under a body.
- **Airdrops.** Once or twice a round, at a random time (fixed by the round's seed, so a replay is exact), the kill feed and minimap announce "Supply drop inbound". A cargo plane's shadow sweeps across the map along a dashed track on the minimap, cuts a crate loose over a random reachable spot, and it floats down on an orange and bone parachute for five seconds. The landed crate wears orange beacon brackets, a SUPPLY DROP plate saying what it gives, and a minimap marker; break it by shooting it (or a blast) within 75 seconds. You get a **golden gun**, which hits 20% harder and gleams for the rest of that life, or, 40% of the time or when your gun is golden already, a full heal, a full magazine, your ability back and 150 points. Either way it is a **Special Delivery**.
- New medals: Kaboom (bronze, 30), Chain Reaction (gold, 100), Special Delivery (silver, 50).
- **Props.** Beside the barrels stand eight more things to shoot (`src/shared/sim/props.ts`, tuned in `PROPS` and `PROP_FX` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, drawn by `src/client/propfx.ts`; map symbols `p g e o l m a i`, which turn about the centre like the barrels and are checked by `node scripts/map-lint.ts` for walls, crates, barrels, zones, spawns within 100 px and a twin of the same kind). Every prop stops bodies and rounds while it stands, wears down under fire (dents, then leaks) and, like a barrel, credits whoever set it off; a blast sets props off too, so a barrel chain can start any of them. They go away for a while and stand again, never under a body, and they exist only on the versus maps (Zombies and Last Squad have none).
  - **Propane tank.** Cream with an orange valve. At zero it vents and **rockets off along the heading of the round that hit it** (away from a blast), tumbling and trailing a flame, and skids with its speed bleeding off until it touches a wall, a crate, a body or another prop, or runs out of its 1.8 seconds, then bursts as a blast of 120 px and up to 95 damage. The kill is a **Liftoff**.
  - **Gas canister.** Olive with a toxic band. Bursts into the gas grenade's cloud (140 px, 14 damage a second dealt as a pulse of 7 every half second, lasting 7 seconds): area denial. The one who shot it is spared, as with the grenade.
  - **Generator** (electrical box). Gunmetal with hazard stripes. Shot to zero it arcs for 0.6 seconds, then pulses an **EMP**: everyone within 210 px (friend or foe) is slowed to 55% for 2.6 seconds and cannot use an ability for 2 seconds, shocked players crackle with arcs, and lamps in reach go dark for a couple of seconds. Killing a player it has shocked, while you are the one who shorted it, is **Shock Therapy**.
  - **Oil drum.** Dark gunmetal with an amber drop. Spills a **burning slick** (100 px, 18 damage a second dealt as a pulse of 9 every half second, 6 seconds) that burns anyone in it but its spiller and glows as a light source. A kill by fire is an **Arsonist**.
  - **Streetlamp.** A tall post with a lamp head and a pool of light. Shoot the bulb and the pool goes dark and the stump sparks; the post stays and blocks, and the bulb comes back 90 seconds later. The simulation tracks only whether it is broken, so everyone sees the same lamp; the light itself is the client's.
  - **Medical cabinet** and **ammo crate.** Walk up to one when you need what it holds (hurt, for a medical cabinet; a magazine not full or your ability cooling, for an ammo crate) and it opens by itself and hands you its pack at once: there is no key, your body coming within 70 px of its centre (pressed to any side or corner, with room to spare) is enough, and the first player in the tick's turn order who needs it gets it. Walk past it at full health (or full of rounds with the ability ready) and it stays shut for whoever needs it. Shoot one open instead and it shatters and leaves a pack (a mint ring, or an amber one) lying 25 seconds. Walk over the health pack when hurt for 50 health, or the ammo pack for a full magazine and your ability ready at once: there is no key, your body brushing the pack takes it. Neither is wasted: a pack is not taken by a player at full health (or with a full magazine and the ability ready), and stays for whoever needs it. Whatever you take pops up over your own soldier, only on your screen, as small toy chips that rise and fade: a mint cross with the health it actually gave ("+12" when you were 12 short), an amber round with the rounds it put back, a spark bolt and READY for the ability, and the same for a supply drop (a gold star for a golden gun). Pickups at the same moment share a row, and a later one lifts the older rows. Bots play by the same rules: one hurt or short of rounds with nobody to fight walks to the nearest pack it needs, or up to a cabinet, which opens for it as for a person (`src/server/bot/supplies.ts`); the client side is `src/client/pickups.ts`.
  - **Paint can.** Shot, it splatters your colour on the floor (a decal that fades over a minute and a half, hurting nobody); a splatter that lands over an enemy is a **Picasso**.
  - Bots path round solid props through the nav grid like barrels, shoot a gas canister, oil drum, generator or a propane tank lined up on an enemy when it would reach one and none of their own, hold fire down a line that meets a hazard beside them, and keep out of fire and gas: out of one they stand in, held at the edge of one on their way.
  - New medals: Liftoff (silver, 50), Shock Therapy (silver, 50), Arsonist (bronze, 40), Picasso (bronze, 25).

Every medal a kill earns is paid and announced together, so one sniper kill from across the map can land Long Shot, One Shot and Eagle Eye at once, each toast lining up beside the last. The weapon medals judge the gun whose round landed the kill.

Every name has a profile that lasts across lives and matches (`src/server/profiles.ts`, saved to `data/profiles.json`): career kills, deaths, matches, best streak, distance walked and how often each medal was earned. Lifetime medals are rungs on 35 career tracks (Centurion for career kills, Marksman for Long Shots, Marathon for km walked, Phantom for Ghost medals, a mastery track for kills with each weapon class such as Sidearm and Sharpshooter, Silencer for One Shots, Scattergun for Two Birds, and so on, plus the eleven Zombies tracks below), each with bronze, silver, gold and platinum tiers; crossing a rung pays 100, 200, 400 or 800 points at once and lands a bigger "Lifetime medal" toast. A player wears their rarest lifetime medal as a badge beside their name in every match. `profile.html?name=...` (linked from the name field, the footer and a human killer on the death card) shows a name's worn medal, career numbers, every versus track with its progress to the next rung, a Zombies section with the run numbers and the Zombies tracks, and every match medal with its count; each track's medal has a hover tooltip naming what counts and every rung; `GET /api/profile/:name` serves the same data. A signed-in account owns its name and its profile: registering a name wipes whatever guests left under it (unless the registrant carries that very profile over, below), a bot or guest holding the name when its owner joins is renamed, a guest can never add to an account's profile, and a second session of the same account still credits the account. Guests sharing an unregistered name share its profile. A guest name with no profile yet makes a new one, at most one per 30 s and five per rolling 24 hours from one IP address (`newProfile*` in `src/server/limits.ts`; kept in memory, so a restart resets it). Past that the guest still plays, but unrecorded: no profile is made or saved for that name, and a chat line suggests registering. Accounts, and guests rejoining under a name that already has a profile, are not counted.

A guest who registers keeps their progress. The guest whose join makes a name's profile is its owner: the server makes a 256-bit secret, keeps only its SHA-256 on the profile (`owner`, never served by `/api/profile`), and hands the token `<base64url name>.<secret>` to that one connection in its `welcome` (`guest`). The page keeps it in localStorage (`skirmish.guestClaims`, one per guest name it made, newest last) and sends the one for the name it last played under with `POST /api/register` as `guest`. The whole profile then moves to the new account: career numbers, medals, lifetime medals and their dates, bests, XP and level, unlocked and equipped cosmetics, challenge progress and the first-win day; the account's stats (`/api/stats`) start from the guest's kills, deaths and games. The reply says `carried: true`, and the sign-up form tells a guest with a claim on file "Your stats and medals come with you." The rules (`reserveClaim` and `commitClaim` in `src/server/profiles.ts`, the register route in `src/server/main.ts`):

- Only the token proves a claim, never a name or id the client sends. A token that is forged, guessed, malformed, for a profile that is gone or already carried over, or for one held by another registration in flight is refused with 403 (`guest: "invalid"`) and no account is made; the page then forgets that token. A second guest sharing the name gets no token, and guest profiles made before claims existed have no owner, so neither can be claimed.
- A profile is carried over once. The move takes it off the guest name and drops its owner, so the token opens nothing again; a guest who takes the name later starts a new profile with a new owner. Guests still seated under the old name stop recording (`Room.forgetGuest`).
- Only registration carries over. `POST /api/login` ignores any `guest` in its body, and a signed-in join never touches a guest profile, so an existing account can never take one.
- The new-profile limit still holds: only a join that passed it makes a profile and a token, carrying one over makes nothing new and refunds nothing, and registering is under the same per-address login limit.
- It is atomic across the two files. The claim is checked and held in the same synchronous step that takes the name. accounts.json is written first (tmp file and rename, as every save) with the account and the claim it took (`claim: { from, owner, at }`); then the profile moves in memory and profiles.json is written at once, not on the 2 s debounce. A crash before the first write changes nothing; a crash between the two is finished at the next start (`Profiles.reconcile`: a recorded claim whose guest profile still has that owner is moved, one whose profile no longer has it is done); and the same pass wipes any guest-owned profile left under an account name, so a registration's wipe that never reached disk is done too.

**Email and password reset.** An account may carry an email, used only to mail a password-reset link (`src/shared/email.ts`, `src/server/accounts.ts`, `src/server/mail.ts`, the routes in `src/server/main.ts`). It is optional: the sign-up form's Email field says "Optional. Only used to reset your password.", and an account without one plays the same but cannot be reset (the forms say "Add an email in your account to enable password reset"). Nothing verifies the address; it is checked for shape only (a plain `local@domain.tld`, at most 254 characters, no quotes, spaces or control characters), trimmed and stored with its domain lowercased, on the client and again on the server.

- It is private. `/api/stats`, `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/profile`, `welcome` and snapshots never carry it; only the account's own session reads it, with `GET /api/account` (`Authorization: Bearer <token>`, answers `{ name, email }`). The signed-in Account panel shows it and a small form adds, changes or removes it with `POST /api/account/email` `{ email, password }` (empty email removes it), which needs the current password (403 `Wrong password`) and kills any reset link already sent.
- Emails need not be unique. A reset is keyed by the account name, so several accounts may share an address and none can learn another account uses it: sign-up never says "email taken".
- "Forgot password?" on the log-in forms takes the account name and its email and calls `POST /api/reset/request` `{ name, email }`. A link goes out only when that name is an account whose email matches the one entered (normalised, ignoring case). Every well-formed request answers 200 with the same message ("If that name and email match an account, we've sent a link…"), whether the name is unknown, the email wrong or the account has no email, and the reply is written before any mail is sent, so neither the answer nor its timing tells them apart. Requests are limited per IP (`resetPerIpPerHour`, 10; past it 429) and reset emails per account (`resetPerAccountPerHour`, 3; past it nothing is sent, the answer is the same).
- The link is `<PUBLIC_URL>/reset?token=<43 base64url chars>` (32 random bytes). The account keeps only the token's SHA-256 and an expiry 30 minutes out (`reset` in accounts.json, so a pending link survives a restart); a new request replaces the old one. `/reset` serves `public/reset.html` with `cache-control: no-store` and `referrer-policy: no-referrer`; its script (`src/client/reset.ts`, built to `public/reset.js`) reads the token, strips it from the address bar with `history.replaceState`, and sends it in the body of `POST /api/reset/confirm` `{ token, password }`. The server compares the hash against each pending reset with `timingSafeEqual`, checks expiry, sets the new password (same 4 to 128 rule as sign-up), deletes the token and sets `validFrom`, so every session token issued before the reset stops naming the account (one already in a match keeps playing until it reconnects). A used, replaced, expired or forged token answers 400.
- The routes that change something (`/api/reset/*`, `/api/account/email`) take only `content-type: application/json` and refuse an `Origin` that is neither the request's host nor `PUBLIC_URL`, and `Sec-Fetch-Site: cross-site`, so another site cannot forge them.
- Mail goes out through Resend (`RESEND_API_KEY` and `MAIL_FROM`, one HTTPS call) or any SMTP server (`SMTP_URL` such as `smtps://user:pass@smtp.example.com:465`, or `smtp://…:587` with STARTTLS required except to localhost, and `MAIL_FROM`), using Node alone. With neither, a reset request answers the same and the server logs that email is not configured. `DEV_MAIL_LOG=1` logs the reset email, link included, instead of sending it, for local testing; it is ignored when `NODE_ENV=production`. The email's HTML escapes every value it shows. Tests pass their own transport (`ServerOptions.mailer`); see `test/password-reset.test.ts`.

### Zombies

Zombies is a co-op mode for a private squad of up to four. Start a squad from the menu's Zombies block and send friends its invite link (`?squad=<code>`), which opens the menu with that squad selected. Under the hood `POST /api/squads` opens a squad room and answers `{ "room": "z-xxxxxx" }`; everyone who joins the WebSocket with `?room=<code>` plays in it. Squads stay off the public room list, one address may open six a minute, at most 20 run at once, and a squad closes 30 seconds after its last human leaves. Bots fill the empty seats and give a seat up when a human joins. Humans keep their fourfold health. The whole squad is one team, and nothing the squad does hurts the squad.

The squad defends the Bastion, a core at the center of the Outpost map with 50 survivors inside. A run opens on a 40-second day, which ends early once every living human presses N. Night follows, and a horde walks in toward the core from the map edges the night table names. Later nights send their packs in waves: night N sends N-1 packs at once, then waits as long as those packs would have taken one at a time. By day the HUD forecasts tonight with the deadliest kinds first and the horde's size for your squad, for example `Tonight · Brutes, walkers and runners from the east · 37 strong`. The night ends once the whole horde has spawned and died. First light comes 90 seconds after the last pack walks in and burns whatever is still out for no scrap, so a zombie that cannot reach anything never holds the night; the phase line counts down to it. `NIGHTS` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists ten nights, one row each, with each kind's count for a squad of four bots and its sides. Night 1 is 24 walkers and a brute from the north; night 2 brings seven brutes, which ignore the squad and march on the core, and from night 3 the horde splits, two or three sides at once and later all four, so a squad standing in one spot cannot hold it and turrets have to cover the sides it is not on. Night 5 brings a Colossus, night 8 another, and night 10, the Tide, brings two and every kind from every side. Each night after the first a zombie has 22% more health than on night 1, bites 12% harder and walks 2.5% faster (to 20% faster by night 9), `ZOM.nightMul`. A human counts as one and a half bots toward the horde's size. A boss, a kind that walks alone like the Colossus, comes once for any squad and has its health scaled by the squad's share instead. Holding through the Tide is the run's great moment, `THE TIDE HELD`, and it pays Held the Line, but the run goes on: only the core's fall ends a run. Past the table the nights are endless (`ENDLESS` and `nightOf` in `src/shared/defs.ts`), and the HUD reads `Night 12 · Endless`. Each is built from the Tide, every kind from every side, with each kind's count 8% bigger a night for ten nights past it and then held, and another Colossus every second night, up to six. Past that the horde grows only in health and bite, by `ZOM.nightMul`, and never more than 200 walk at once (`ZOM.maxAlive`): a wave brings only the packs that fit under that cap, and the rest wait for room. Losing the last survivor ends nothing either. The fallen are then not sent back at night and wait for dawn, which stands every squad player up free, while the core fights on with its turrets until first light. When the core falls the run is over: `The Bastion fell on night N`, or, for a squad that held the Tide on the way, `The Tide held. The Bastion fell on night N, M past the Tide.` A report of the night reached, the run's length, each player's kills, revives and buildings put up, and the kills of the turrets and the Bastion shows for 20 seconds, and then a fresh run starts on a reset map.

Every 100 hp of harm the core takes costs one survivor, however well the core is mended between bites, so repairing the core keeps it standing but raises no one. At night the HUD shows tonight's dead beside the survivor count. At dawn each survivor left pays 3 scrap. The survivors also shoot from the core's walls at zombies within 220 px, slower as they die.

A walker bites a squad player it can see within 160 px. Runners are small and fast, come in packs and chase the squad from 360 px. Plated walkers shrug 10 damage off every bullet, so small rounds barely scratch them while heavy rounds, piercing rounds and blasts get through. A bloater bursts where it dies, hurting the squad, the horde, every building within 110 px and the core if it is that close, so it is best killed far off. A brute and the Colossus, a far bigger brute, ignore the squad and march on the core. Otherwise a zombie bites the core once it reaches it, and the core shrugs off 40% of each bite. On the way it follows a flow field to the core that goes round the squad's walls while there is an open way and breaks through the cheapest wall when there is not. Zombie kills pay score up the same level ladder as the versus modes, and each kill adds scrap to the squad's shared bank: 3 for a walker, 2 for a runner, 7 for a plated walker, 6 for a bloater, 15 for a brute and 80 for a Colossus, on top of the 140 a run opens with. Scrap only buys buildings, upgrades, repairs and rounds, so a squad that never builds banks it for nothing. A player's level, perks and gun last for the whole run.

Every gun has a job against the horde, in line with its job in versus (`ZOMBIE_CLASS_ROLES` and `zombieRole` in `src/shared/defs.ts`, applied in `src/shared/sim/zomroles.ts`; none of it reaches a versus room or a player). A player's own kill pays the zombie's scrap times the bounty of the gun in their hand, `zombieBounty`: the middle gun's harm a second to the horde over this gun's, to the 0.05, held between 0.8 and 1.8, from `HORDE_DPS`, each gun's harm a second in real runs as `scripts/bench-zombie-guns.ts` measures it. So the slowest killers build fastest: the shotgun pays 1.5 times the scrap a kill, the Akimbo, Double Barrel and Street Sweeper 1.35, the pistol 1.25 and the SMG 1.1, while the carbine, battle rifle, repeater, minigun and juggernaut pay 0.8. A turret's kill and the Bastion's pay the plain price, and each `zkill` event carries what the kill paid. On top of that:

| Class | Against the horde |
| --- | --- |
| Pistol | Field mechanic: repairs buildings and reloads turrets with use 60% faster (the core at the usual rate), and 1.5 times the harm to runners |
| SMG | Runner hunter: 1.75 times the harm to runners, and repairs buildings and reloads turrets 30% faster |
| Shotgun | Crowd breaker: each hit staggers a zombie to 35% of its pace for 0.7 s and shoves twice as hard, and walkers and runners take 1.8 times the harm |
| Assault | Long-range anchor: bloaters, best popped far off, take 1.8 times the harm, and plating takes only half its 10 off each round |
| Sniper | Big game: each round passes through 3 zombies before the gun's own piercing counts, and brutes, plated walkers and the Colossus take 1.3 times the harm (1.2 from the quick-firing Semi-auto, Ghost and Repeater) |
| LMG | Suppressor: each hit holds a zombie to 70% of its pace for 0.45 s, and its fast killers pay the least scrap |

A few evolutions do their own job: the Rail Slug pierces 3 zombies and hits the heavies 1.4 times as hard, the Executioner pierces 2 and hits them 1.3 times as hard, the Ripper pierces 2, and the Boom Slug, Grenadier and Artillery blast the horde 1.3 times as hard but a plated walker or the Colossus takes only half of a blast. The strongest hold on a zombie wins, and brutes and the Colossus, which take no shove, shrug off holds too. With the guns this much stronger against the horde, a player's gun deals the horde 72% of its harm (`HORDE_GUN_MUL`, before the multipliers above; turrets keep all of theirs), which keeps a squad that never builds falling on night 3 while a building squad holds as long as before. In Zombies the gear step's gun tiles, the evolve pick and the HUD's loadout strip (where the gun's tile shows from the start, with its bounty as a chip such as `+50%`) say each gun's bounty and job.

By day a player presses B for build mode and puts up a building on a 50 px grid cell within 600 px of the core and 250 px of themselves, on a cell clear of cover, the core, bodies and other buildings. The build bar lists what can go up in three rows, walls by tier, turrets and utilities, and the keys 1 to 9, or a click or tap on a chip, pick it: 1 the wall, 2 a sentry (70 scrap), 3 a cannon (180), 4 a scatter (90), 5 a mortar (220), 6 a tesla coil (260), 7 an ammo depot (90), 8 a repair post (110), 9 a spike strip (12). The wall comes in three tiers, picked on the bar's top row or by pressing 1 again, Q or the mouse wheel (which steps through everything): a barricade of boards for 10 scrap and 800 hp, a sandbag wall for 24 and 2000 hp, and a steel wall for 60 and 4800 hp. Sandbags shrug off 10% of every bite and take 0.75 of a bloater's burst, steel 30% and half, and a barricade mends fastest. With a wall or a spike strip picked, pressing on a cell and dragging lays a line of them: it runs straight from that cell along whichever way the drag went farther, across or up and down, up to 12 cells, and the ghost shows every cell with its price, green where it will go up and red where it cannot (out of reach, blocked, taken, or past what the bank can pay), with the line's count and total on its plate. Letting go builds it in one message (`{ t: 'build', kind, cells }`), the server putting each cell up in order from where the drag began by the same rules as a single build, so the line stands as far as the scrap lasts and a cell that cannot take it is passed over; Esc or a right click drops the line, and a press let go where it began builds the one. Build mode needs a keyboard: on a phone there is no B, so phones cannot build. A building can come down by day for half of everything put into it back, less the share of it that is worn. Buildings block bodies, zombies included, but the squad's bullets and grenades pass over them, and a spike strip, which lies flat, blocks nobody. A sentry and a mortar have 1000 hp, a scatter 1200, a cannon 1500, a tesla coil 1100, a depot 900, a post 800 and a strip 450. A walker bites a building for half its damage and a brute for its full damage.

By day anything but a spike strip can be upgraded where it stands, up to level 3, for scrap: U upgrades the building under the cursor in build mode (or, outside it, the nearest one within 250 px, with a gold hint naming the step and its price), so does the build bar's upgrade chip, and so does a left click on a standing building that can step up. Hovering one in build mode shows its name, level, health, the next step's price and what taking it down pays. A wall steps from barricade to sandbag to steel for the difference of their prices (14, then 36). Anything else pays 65% of its price for level 2 and a further 115% for level 3. A turret's level 2 and 3 fire 1.4 and 1.9 times as hard, 0.85 and 0.7 times as far apart, reach 10% and 20% farther and hold 1.5 and 2.2 times the load (a mortar's burst grows with its damage); every building has 1.4 and 1.9 times the health, and a depot's and a post's work goes 1.6 and 2.4 times as fast and 15% and 30% farther. An upgrade keeps the building's share of health and load, and the turret's builder keeps the credit for its kills. A turret is a toy emplacement sandbagged onto the floor, its gun turning on a turntable to aim (`src/client/turretart.ts`), and it looks its level: steel plates bolted over the sandbags at level 2 and a riveted steel collar with hazard chevrons at level 3, more barrels (a sentry has two, three, then four under a perforated shroud, a scatter a fan of three, five, then seven, a mortar one, two, then three tubes), side shields, recoil cylinders, a muzzle brake's evacuator and a sighting optic, and gold pips in its corner. Its gun kicks back when it fires, with a flash at the barrel that fired and a warm pool on the floor; a lamp on its corner glows amber, blinks when the load runs low and red when dry, over a five-cell ammo gauge; and it shows its wear in soot, torn and missing sandbags and holed plates. A turret's range is a thin dashed ring of exactly the reach it aims within: round the build ghost before it goes up, round a built one under the cursor (in build mode, or by day outside it, else the one U would upgrade), with the next level's reach in dashed amber beside it by day. A sentry's, cannon's or scatter's ring is shaded where the map's walls and crates hide the floor from it, a mortar's (it lobs over cover and has no minimum range) and a coil's (it arcs through it) are whole. At night a faint dotted edge shows what each kind of the squad's turrets covers together (Settings, Turret ranges at night). Squad bots with no human in the squad build the ring of twelve turrets round the Bastion as before, but step the turret nearest the side tonight's horde comes from up to level 2 once nine stand, then add the last three turrets, an ammo depot and a repair post behind that side's guns, a line of four sandbag walls across its way (later steel), spike strips ahead of the line, a tesla coil beside it, and level 2 and 3 for every turret, the ones facing the horde first.

A turret fires real rounds at the nearest zombie in range that no map wall or crate hides, and the squad's own buildings never block its view. Each turret prefers one kind of zombie and fires at the nearest of that kind first. A turret whose round would lose more than half to plating leaves plated zombies and the Colossus to heavier guns. A sentry reaches 420 px and fires seven 14-damage rounds a second, so it thins walkers. A cannon reaches 560 px and fires one 260-damage round every 2.2 seconds at brutes first. A scatter reaches 260 px and fires seven 11-damage pellets a shot at runners first. A mortar reaches 750 px and lobs a shell every 2.6 seconds over map cover to where its target will be when it lands, bursting for up to 160 damage across 120 px, plated first, so it breaks up packs. A tesla coil reaches 230 px and arcs a bolt every second from itself to its target for 40 damage, then on to the nearest zombie within 120 px of each, up to three more, each jump 0.75 as hard as the last, through cover and plating alike and with no round to fly; its builder gets the credit. A sentry holds 120 rounds, a scatter 40, a cannon 10, a mortar 8 and a coil 36, and an empty turret stops firing. Its rounds never hurt the squad. A turret's kill pays the bank its scrap and pays the turret's builder the score, but counts as the turret's kill, not the builder's.

Utilities work beside the guns. An ammo depot tops up the neediest turret within 175 px, 6% of that turret's load a second, for 0.6 of the usual scrap, and reloads the gun of any squad player standing there. A repair post mends every squad player within 175 px 4 health a second and every building there 14, free, day and night. A spike strip is floor: zombies walk over it on a flow field that ignores it, slowed to 45% of their step and hurt 14 a second while they stand on it, and wear it down 7 hp a second each (24 for a brute, bloater or the Colossus) until it is gone. Holding use repairs the nearest damaged building or the core within 250 px at 80 hp a second, or reloads the nearest turret short of ammo, day or night. A worn turret is repaired before it is reloaded. The `Hold E to ...` hint names the job by the same rule the server follows. Repairing a building costs half its price for the share of it that is worn, so mending always costs less than taking it down and building it again, and the core costs 1 scrap per 5 hp. A sentry round costs 0.25 scrap, a scatter round 0.5, a cannon round 4 and a mortar round 5, and a full reload takes 2.5 seconds.

A player whose health runs out goes down instead of dying. A downed player crawls, cannot shoot and is ignored by the horde. A squadmate holding use within 70 px for 3 seconds revives them with 40% health. A downed player nobody revives within 25 seconds bleeds out. By night the Bastion sends them back 15 seconds later at the cost of survivors, 1 plus half the night's number rounded up, even the very last of them, and with too few survivors left they wait for dawn. A human who joins or rejoins during the night sits out until dawn. Bots fight, revive, keep a dry turret firing and post behind the squad's walls, finding their way round the core and the squad's buildings. In a squad with no human they also repair and reload while no zombie is close and put up a fixed ring of turrets by day, the same distance out on every side, saving the scrap for each before they mend the core. With a human in the squad the bank is the humans': bots build nothing, keep enough for the dearest building, tend only with what is above that, and mend the core below it only when it falls under half at night.

**Zombies lifetime medals.** Eleven of the lifetime tracks count Zombies play (`ZOM_STATS` and the `z...` rows of `CAREER` in `src/shared/defs.ts`), in the horde's olive and lime, each with an emblem of its own (`src/client/medals.ts`). They work like every other lifetime medal: the toast, the pay, the worn badge and the unlocks. Bronze comes in a first run or two and platinum takes months:

The platinum Night Owl, Held the Line, Bricklayer and Titan Toppler unlock the Night Owl title, the First Light name colour, the Hard Hat helmet and the Titan Toppler title. Only a seated human's own play counts, signed in or a recorded guest, like every other career stat: bots keep no profile, the range records nothing and versus rooms add nothing to these tracks. The room reads what the run already says (`src/server/zomcareer.ts` and `payRun` and `creditProfiles` in `src/server/room.ts`): its kill, revive, shot and hit events, each accepted build and upgrade, the health each player mended (`Run.mended`, the one field the simulation keeps for it) and the core's harm. A night counts, toward Night Owl, Last Light and Not a Scratch, for whoever was seated from its dusk, the same rule as its XP. Rounds and mended health are paid to the profile in lumps of 25 and 250, the rest when the phase turns or the player leaves.

### Last Squad

Last Squad, the BR room, is a battle royale for six squads of three. Each squad wears one of the six colors, and a squad's bullets and blasts spare its squadmates. A match plays one of the four versus maps, the next map in the rotation each time. Each squad starts together, at the spot farthest from the squads already placed.

The whole map is safe for the first 60 seconds. Then the safe circle closes in six phases, each new circle inside the last and centred on open ground: to 3200 px over 30 seconds, then after 45 seconds to 2500 px over 25, after 40 seconds to 1900 px over 20, after 30 seconds to 1300 px over 20, after 25 seconds to 700 px over 15, and after 20 seconds to nothing over 15. Outside the circle a player loses 2% of their max health a second in the first phase, then 3, 5, 8, 12 and 20%, as a pulse of half that every half second on the world clock. The ring burns through armor and the spawn shield, and nobody outside it regenerates. `RING` in `src/shared/defs.ts` lists the phases.

A player whose health runs out while a squadmate is still standing is knocked, not killed. The knock pays the kill: 100 points, the bounty on a hunted player and the assists, as in the other modes. A knocked player crawls, cannot shoot, and has half their max health as a knocked pool that enemies' bullets and blasts take away to finish them, and the ring wears it down too. A squadmate holding E within 70 px for 3 seconds revives them with 40% health. Nobody in 25 seconds and they bleed out. A squad is out the moment nobody in it is standing, and its knocked players die with it. The kill feed tags each knock and names each finish, redeploy and squad that goes out.

Until the third phase closes, a dead player comes back beside a standing squadmate after 15 seconds, 10 seconds longer for each earlier death in the match, with their class gun and a spawn shield. When the third phase closes the HUD announces Last lives, and nobody redeploys after that. A dead player's camera follows a squadmate who is still up, then whoever killed them, then anyone left in the match.

Each phase drops supply 20 seconds into its wait, on open ground inside the next circle, and every minimap shows where it will land with a countdown from 10 seconds before. A drop is a gold crate with 300 health under a SUPPLY DROP plate that says what it gives. Whoever breaks it jumps straight to their next level pick and gets full health, a full magazine and their ability back as well; a LEVEL UP chip and the rest pop up over them, and the kill feed says who cracked it. Crates pay 25 points and stay broken for the rest of the match.

The last squad standing wins. Squads that go out in the same tick place by their kills, more kills placing higher. A squad that is out sees its result card: `#1 · Last squad standing` or `#4 of 6`, with your kills, knocks and revives. The next match starts 8 seconds after the win. A human who joins while redeploys are open takes the place of a bot in the squad with the fewest humans, where it stood and in the state it was in, so solo humans spread one per squad. A human who leaves then hands the place back to a bot. After the third phase closes a joiner watches until the next match seats them.

The pill shows the ring's phase and the time to its next move. Six columns of three pips under it track every squad: filled while up, hollow while knocked, faint while dead, and the squad's place once it is out. The world and the minimap shade everything outside the circle and dash the next one, and the minimap shows the drops and counts the ring down.

Bots play the versus brain, held to their squad. A squad's anchor is the middle of its standing members, pulled to 400 px inside the circle's edge, or to 60% of the radius on a circle under 1000 px. Each bot holds cover within 220 px of the anchor, facing the circle's centre, and drops any plan of its own that goes farther, so bots never chase gunfire or flank across the map. Bots set off for the next circle once the wait left is only 15 seconds more than the walk, and a bot caught outside walks back in whatever it is fighting. A bot revives a knocked squadmate within 900 px once no standing enemy is in sight, and shoots knocked enemies once no standing enemy is in sight. About half the squads go for each phase's drop when it lands within 1800 px of them.

### Shooting range

The range (mode `RNG`) is a practice room for one: a private lane where you can test any gun, any evolution, any armor and any perk and ability against targets at known distances. Open it from the menu's Shooting range card, which calls `POST /api/range` and answers `{ "room": "r-xxxxxx" }`; the room takes one human, has no bots, stays off the public list (`/api/servers`), and closes 30 seconds after its player leaves. One address may open a range as often as a squad (six a minute) and at most 40 run at once. `createRoom` treats `RNG` as a practice room (`src/server/range.ts`): nothing in it touches an account, a profile, XP, a medal or a career track (`test/range.test.ts` runs a room against a profile that fails on any call). Nothing hurts you there, you are never hunted, no level or pick prompt ever opens, and kills and score stay at zero.

The Range map (`src/shared/maps/range.ts`, 2400 px square) is kept sparse, in the spirit of a modern shooter's firing range: a firing line on the west edge and one open **field** running east, with the distances painted across it from the line at 100, 200, 400, 700, 1000 and 1250 px, and one target standing at each: paper soldiers at 100, 200 and 400 and wood boards at 700, 1000 and 1250. They step along a bowed diagonal, near ones south of the pad and far ones north, so from anywhere on the line beside the pad none stands in front of another (`test/range.test.ts` checks it). The bolt-action and the rifles reach the 1000 board, and only the farthest-reaching snipers carry to the last; `test/range.test.ts` shoots every target from the firing line. Two narrow bays flank the field: **Moving** to the north, with two paper soldiers on carts sliding across the line of fire at 400 px (170 px/s) and 700 px (300 px/s), and **Blast** to the south, with a training dummy of 600 health at 200 px for reading damage per second and a small blast corner past it: a low wall with a paper soldier behind it to lob a grenade over, two explosive barrels, a propane tank and an oil drum. The booth is a concrete back wall with a doorway by the pad and a short sandstone divider at each bay's edge, behind the line.

Targets are real simulation entities (`src/shared/sim/targets.ts`, held on `World.range`, which only a Range world has, so the other modes and the golden replay are untouched). Each has health (`TARGETS` in `src/shared/range.ts`: paper 100, plank 260, rail 100, dummy 600) and is hurt through the normal damage paths: a round meets it in `moveBullet` (and loses damage to its gun's falloff as a body would), a blast in `explode` (with a wall sheltering it as it shelters a body), a knife lunge through the ability, and gas, fire and land mines in `tickRange`. Hits raise the usual `dmg` events (`kind: 'target'`), so you get damage numbers, hit markers and sounds like a real hit, and a kill raises a `target` event with `by` for the kill marker. A target that falls lies `RANGE.regenMs` (4 s), then stands again whole; one that is hurt and left alone heals after 4 s; barrels and props stand again 6 s after they go off. A slider's place is a pure function of the server clock (`targetPos`), so it costs the wire nothing, and a lagged shot is judged where it stood when the shooter saw it. The snapshot carries each target's health in tenths (sticky `targets`), and nothing else about them.

The readout (`Snapshot.range`) shows the last hit's damage and distance, damage per second over the last 3 seconds, accuracy (trigger pulls that hit at least once, so a shotgun's eight pellets are one hit, over trigger pulls) and the time to kill the last target you dropped, from its first hit, with its distance. Its Reset button, like the loadout panel's, sends `{ t: 'range', a: 'reset' }`, which starts the readout over and stands every target, barrel and prop up whole.

The gun you hold marks its reach on the floor (`src/client/rangeline.ts`): a dotted signal-orange arc where its rounds die, centred on you, since a round leaves the muzzle `MUZZLE_PX` ahead of your centre and flies its range (after perks such as Long range) in whatever direction you aim. It follows you as you walk, so it is honest from anywhere, and on the firing line its far point sits just past the painted distance of the same number. A plate at the far point reads, say, `MAX RANGE 1200 · BOLT-ACTION`, and pins to the screen's edge with a chevron when the far point is off it. A gun with damage falloff also paints a faint band between where the falloff starts and where it bottoms out, and the plate's second line reads `FALLOFF 180–380 → 30% DMG`. A change of gun or perk stamps the plate and slides the arc to its new reach in 200 ms. Each standing target carries a chip under its base: an orange tick when a round from where you stand reaches it at full damage, half orange in the falloff, a dark cross when the round dies short. A target is reached when its edge is, as a round meets it. `test/range-reach.test.ts` fires real rounds to prove the arc, the plate and every chip.

L (or the Loadout button) opens the loadout panel: every gun with its evolutions in a column per class, the four armors, and every perk of tiers 1 to 3 with a None chip to clear a tier. A click sends `{ t: 'range', a: 'loadout', gun?, armor?, perks?: { 1?, 2?, 3? } }` (a tier left out stays as it is, `null` clears it). The server takes it only in a Range room, parses and validates every id against its own tier (`parseClientMsg`, then `validLoadout` again), refuses it anywhere else with an error message, and applies it at once with a full health bar, magazine and ability. Any tier-1 perk goes on any gun, whether or not the class lists it as an attachment. The panel and readout (`src/client/rangeui.ts`, `public/range.css`) wear the field kit and sit in the HUD, so `--ui` scales them; the targets, their fall and spring and the floor markings are `src/client/targetart.ts`.

### Music and radios

The soundtrack is played live in the browser in one adaptive framework: a calm layer (always), a combat layer that swells with the fight, a hype layer (on a streak, a multi-kill or being hunted), a finale layer (the last 60 seconds, or a boss) and, in the Zombies night, a heartbeat layer. Drums, synth leads and basses are synthesized (oscillators, filtered noise, envelopes; among them an 808 that glides, a phonk cowbell, a synth pluck, an eight-bit triangle bass, a riser and an impact for the drops); the acoustic and electric instruments are short samples from the Fluid R3 General MIDI soundfont (MIT licence, notice in `public/music/LICENSE.txt`), a few notes each as small mono MP3s in `public/music/` (`node scripts/build-music-samples.ts` rebuilds them). A track loads only its own samples when it starts, and its synth stand-ins play until they arrive (`musicsamples.ts`). Kills ring a sting built on the chord that is playing, so it is always in key, and a round's end plays a win or loss cadence on the track's tonic. **Shift+M** turns the music off and on; **M** mutes everything. The map id the server sends picks the track, and a map change crossfades to the new map's track over 3.5 s at a bar line (`music.ts`: two decks, equal-power curves). The menu and the Plaza play the original Toy March (`musicclassic.ts`, restored from the first soundtrack).

Every other map plays its own synthesized theme, written to a few pop-songwriting rules (`docs/music/CRAFT.md`): one short, rhythmic hook that keeps coming back, one proven four-chord loop, and an arc that builds in layers. An 8-bar intro (beat and pad, then the bass with the hook teased) leads into a 32-bar loop of four sections: the hook, a call-and-response groove (the lead jumps an octave in a fight), a breakdown and build (a riser, then a silent beat), and the drop (an impact, the big bass and the hook; half-time on an 808 for the trap and phonk themes). After the drop the loop comes round to the hook. Something changes every 8 bars (fills, octave jumps, the counter-melody, the comping), and every other time round the drop doubles the hook. The calm layer is a small, complete version of the song with the hook always in it; a fight adds the backbeat, the full kit and the lead; a streak adds the counter-melody; the finale adds sixteenth hats and a harmony. A theme is a short `PopSpec` (`musicthemes.ts`: the hook and counter-melody as notes, bass riffs as chord degrees, the band and the beat as patterns), arranged bar by bar by `musicpop.ts`; nothing about the tune depends on the seed, which only picks fills.

A round through a radio makes it sputter for a moment. Bots ignore radios, and a radio has no collision.

On every versus map (FFA, TDM, DOM, Last Squad) six to eight smaller **hidden radios** (at least 900 px apart where the map allows, never under 600) sit tucked into corners and alcoves, re-rolled every round: `scripts/radio-pool.ts` picks 30 spots per map (walkable and reachable from a spawn by the map-lint rasters, a wall within 100 px on a run of three to five compass directions and never on opposite sides, 220 px clear of spawn pads and zone-radius plus 160 px clear of zones) into `src/shared/radiopool.ts`, and each client picks from them with a generator seeded by the map and the round's name (`match.roundEndsAt`, or `royale.round` in Last Squad), so every player sees the same radios and the sim's random numbers are untouched. A hidden radio retunes only you (client only, no message): the tracks, "Map default" and Off, until the round ends. The first radio found in a session says "Found a radio!"; finds are counted (`radioFinds()`) for a possible future challenge, with no XP or medal yet.

## Progression

Every human has an account-level profile (a signed-in account's, or a guest's under their name) that levels up and unlocks things to show off. None of it touches the simulation: it lives in `src/server/profiles.ts`, `src/server/progression.ts` and `src/server/room.ts`, with the catalog and rules shared in `src/shared/cosmetics.ts` and `src/shared/challenges.ts` (the contract is documented at the top of `cosmetics.ts`).

- **Levels.** Level 1 at 0 XP; going from level L to L+1 takes 400 + 60 x L XP (level 25 is 27,600 XP, level 60 is about 130,000, level 100 about 337,000). The pace is tuned for a casual player at roughly 3,000 XP an hour: about level 25 to 30 after 10 hours, level 60 after 50. Past level 100, XP keeps counting and every further 10,000 XP is a **prestige star**, shown next to the name. Existing profiles start at level 1 with 0 XP and are given the cosmetics their lifetime medals already earned.
- **XP sources.** At the end of each life: 1 XP per 5 score and 25 per kill, the life capped at 750 XP. At the end of a round (when you were seated for at least a minute): +40 for finishing and +100 for winning (FFA first place, the winning team, the last squad). Zombies: +60 per night survived, +300 for holding the Bastion (in place of the +100), +40 for finishing the run. The first round won each UTC day doubles that round-end award. Each completed challenge pays its own XP.
- **Cosmetics.** Six slots: helmet, camo, gun skin, name colour, title and kill effect, about 100 items in all, each common, rare, epic or legendary. They unlock by account level (something at every level up to 50, rarer ones from 55 to 100), by lifetime medal rung (Centurion Crest, Phantom Hood, the Giant Slayer title and so on) or only from a weekly challenge. Each slot has a default that is always available. A player wears what they equip: signed-in players equip with `POST /api/equip` from the menu or the `equip` socket message in a match, guests send their picks with `join`, and the server only accepts items the profile has unlocked. Others see it through `PlayerView.cos`. Bots wear a random common or rare set that is fixed by their name.
- **Challenges.** Each profile gets 3 daily challenges per UTC day and 3 weekly ones per ISO week, chosen from the date and the profile so they are the same all day but differ between players: "Get 3 Long Shots", "Win a round", "Survive 2 zombie nights" and about thirty more. Dailies pay 150 to 400 XP, weeklies 1,000 to 2,000, and one weekly also unlocks a challenge-only cosmetic. Progress comes from the same events profiles already count: kills, weapon kills, medals, matches, rounds won, streaks, zombie nights.
- **API and messages.** `GET /api/profile/:name` adds `level`, `xp`, `xpInLevel`, `xpToNext`, `prestige`, `unlocked`, `equipped` and `challenges` (with texts and reset times). The server sends the player a `progress` message after a life or round that earned XP or unlocked anything, and once on joining.

## Layout

- `src/shared/defs.ts` holds every tuning number: weapons, armor, perks, cooldowns and world constants.
- `src/shared/maps.ts` registers the maps and each mode's rotation; a map's `size` is its side in pixels. The versus maps live in `src/shared/maps/`, each drawn as a text grid of 50 px cells: the west half only, since `src/shared/mapgrid.ts` turns it half way round for the east and merges cells into wall, spawn and crate rects. Its legend is `.` floor, `#` concrete, `S` sandstone, `P` planter, `c` crate, `b` explosive barrel, `p` propane tank, `g` gas canister, `e` generator, `o` oil drum, `l` streetlamp, `m` medical cabinet, `a` ammo crate, `i` paint can, `R` red spawn (blue once turned), `F` FFA spawn, `X` both, and `A` zone A (B is the centre, C is A turned). Outpost, the zombies map, is still drawn in rects. `node scripts/map-lint.ts` checks every map for walls or crates past the edge, crates on walls, ground nobody can walk to, spawns a player cannot stand in, team spawns in sight of each other, a broken half turn and misplaced DOM zones, and lists each map's longest clear sightline against the longest gun's reach; `test/map-lint.test.ts` fails on any problem.
- `src/shared/protocol.ts` defines the wire messages and parses client input.
- `src/shared/wire.ts` encodes snapshots per connection: it rounds numbers and omits crates, leaderboard, zones and match while they are unchanged. The client rebuilds full snapshots from the last one it received.
- `src/shared/sim.ts` is the deterministic game simulation's entry point. `step` advances the world one tick, and the player commands add, remove, respawn and steer players. The rest lives in `src/shared/sim/`, one module per domain:
  - `world.ts` holds the world and player types, the seeded random source, map loading and spawn points.
  - `movement.ts` holds the collision geometry and player motion that the client also runs for prediction.
  - `stats.ts` holds perks, levels and score, and the effective stats they produce.
  - `combat.ts` holds bullets, damage, kills and lag compensation. Each input carries the server time of the world the client was drawing, and a shot first flies through that past, up to the client's measured round trip plus its render delay and never more than `MAX_REWIND_MS` back, so players hit what they aim at on screen.
  - `abilities.ts` holds the tier 3 abilities and the grenades, mines and gas they leave behind.
  - `dot.ts` holds the pulse clock every damage over time lands on (gas clouds, burning slicks, the Last Squad ring): one hit every 500 ms to whoever is in it then, the first 500 ms after the source starts, at the same damage a second, so a body shows one number and one hit sound a pulse. Spike strips in the horde mode still hurt every step, as they slow and wear by the step and show no numbers.
  - `barrels.ts` and `props.ts` hold the explosive barrels and the other shootable props (tanks, canisters, generators, drums, lamps, cabinets, paint): their health, what they do when shot to zero, the clouds, slicks and EMPs they leave, pickups and respawns. `propview.ts` is the footprint of a prop as the wire shows it, which the client predicts against.
  - `modes.ts` holds the FFA, TDM, DOM, zombies, Last Squad and shooting range rules and the round cycle.
  - `targets.ts` holds the shooting range: its targets, how every weapon hurts them, their regeneration, the readout and the loadout a range player may put on.
  - `royale.ts` holds Last Squad: the ring's phases, knocks and finishes, redeploys, supply drops, squads going out, the win, seats for joiners and whom the dead watch.
  - `downed.ts` holds the downed life both squad modes share: going down, the revive and bleeding out.
  - `run.ts` holds the zombies run: its day, night and restart cycle, the night table's hordes, survivors, ready-up, the win, reinforcements, walls and scrap.
  - `build.ts` holds the building rules, which the server applies and the client's build preview mirrors: what can go up where, prices and levels (a wall's tier, a turret's stats at a level, upgrade prices, what taking one down pays back) and what holding use tends. `utility.ts` holds what the utilities do beside the horde's tick: spike strips slowing, hurting and wearing, steel walls turning aside bites, the depot and the post.
  - `horde.ts` holds the zombies themselves: the flow field to the core, their steps, bites and spacing.
  - `snapshot.ts` builds each player's culled view of the world, centred on the player a dead Last Squad player watches.
- `src/server/` contains rooms, bots, accounts and the HTTP and WebSocket server. `bots.ts` runs each bot's think. `bot/` holds its parts:
  - `arena.ts` builds each world's nav grid (`nav.ts`, A* over a grid of where a body fits) and cover points (`cover.ts`, spots beside walls and crates with the bearings they shield) once per wall layout.
  - `awareness.ts` turns a bot's snapshot into what it knows: enemies in sight (in its `botSight` box, a person's 16:9 screen leaned toward its aim, see View), where it last saw others, hunted pings and Tracker marks on its minimap, and gunfire it heard (`heard`, from `heardShots` in `src/shared/sim/hearing.ts`: roughly placed, within the `BOT_HEARING` earshot). A bot shot at from off its screen turns to where the rounds come from (`unseenShooter`), as a person reads the tracers and the gunfire, and then answers that shooter first. A bot counts as on a human's screen (and thinks at the full pace) within the most that human's 16:9 screen could show at any aim: his view radius across and the screen's height, each widened by his gun's full lean (`screenReach` in `tick.ts`).
  - `intent.ts` holds the personalities, each weapon class's fight range, and the one transition table between intents (patrol, take position, engage, peek and hide, hold, reload in cover, retreat and heal, flank, search). An intent holds for a minimum time unless an interrupt fires.
  - `tactics.ts` is a bot's read of the fight, from nothing but what its screen shows (`docs/bots/GAP.md` lists what a good player does that bots did not, and where each is now handled). It remembers how it last saw each enemy (where, facing where, holding still or moving, reloading: every screen animates a reload), and with nobody in sight but someone about it pre-aims where he would come from, whatever it is doing: the edge of the cover he went behind, on his side of it, or the way a shot came (`watchPoint`); one who steps into that angle is taken in in 0.6 of its usual reaction time. It weighs each fight (`fightOdds`: the log of his time to kill it over its time to kill him, from both health bars as a share of full, both guns at that range, both armors, who has whom in front of his gun, reloads, a sprint's lowered gun, a slow gun that has just fired at it and is still cycling, its own suppression and post-sprint bloom, and every other enemy that can get on it against the mates by it), and takes it only above its temper's `takesOdds` (aggressive -0.45, marksman -0.2, cautious -0.05; a hunted enemy on odds 1 worse). Below that it breaks his line for cover hidden from every enemy it sees and holds the angle he would come from (`hold`, 2.5 to 4.5 s, then it goes round another way and takes what it finds); a fight in hand that sinks 0.3 under the line (a second enemy coming up on it, its health gone) is left at once, never a 1v2, unless the one it fights is nearly dead; one well in hand (odds over 0.6) is pushed rather than peeked, while one not clearly its own (under 0.3) seen coming, out of his fire, is met from cover with a lane on him and poked (and poked more often once in it), leaving that cover to push only at 0.9 (he is caught reloading, nearly dead or outnumbered). It shoots first whoever is shooting it, then the most dangerous, then the one it drops soonest (low health, reloading, aimed at it, near). From cover it pokes: shot at a quarter second into a peek it ducks back, a hide is cut short to catch a reload it saw start, a peek into an angle the enemy was last seen holding (planted, aimed within 15 degrees of it) is put off twice and then flanked, and a lost enemy last seen holding the way it would come is flanked or held rather than chased round his corner. With an enemy about it reloads only out of his line unless under 20% of its magazine. `node scripts/bench-freeze.ts` measures bots frozen facing away from every known threat, how often they face one, pokes, time outnumbered, deaths outnumbered, fights lost and life length.
  - `royale.ts` holds a Last Squad bot to its squad's anchor inside the ring, and has it revive and crawl.
  - `motor.ts` turns the intent into keys, aim and fire. No bot turns back within 400 ms of its last turn back. `aim.ts` holds the reaction and aim model: the gun turns toward where the bot wants it on a capped spring, the aim error drifts with a 400 ms time constant, and a bot fires only once its gun is on target. A bot takes in an enemy 220 to 350 ms after it comes into sight (`noticeMs`), scaled by its temper (`Personality.reactMul`: aggressive 0.8, marksman 0.85, cautious 0.95), and turns toward one caught well off its facing on a quicker hand (`HANDS.startle`) meanwhile. `BOT_AIM.errMul` (0.82) scales every bot's aim error: how steady its hand is. Its gun is a person's: the same spread, bloom and bloom recovery (there is no bot multiplier on any of them). A tapping gun's bot (`fireRhythm`, `holdsFire` in `motor.ts`) reads its own bloom off the rounds leaving its magazine and fires once the cone at the enemy's range fits him about as well as a settled gun would (`GOOD_SHOT`, 0.9 of the rested odds, a crossing enemy's lead counting against both), or once its patience since its last round runs out (the gun's pause times the temper's `commitMul`, at most 1.6 s: a bolt-action about 1.0 s for a hothead to 1.6 s for a marksman), and at once inside `TAP_FROM_PX`, when it is being shot or when the enemy is about to walk out of its sight. Assault-class guns keep their tap rhythm, the pause ending as soon as the next tap would land.
  - Bots are not all equally good. Each has a skill (`BOT_SKILL` in `aim.ts`), hashed from its name and the room's seed (`skillFor`), so the same name in the same room is the same player across its lives and each time it joins: a quarter are rookies, 55% regulars and a fifth veterans, with a continuous value inside each band. Skill is the brain only; a rookie carries a person's gun, body and health like any bot (`test/bot-parity.test.ts`). The rookie row has 2.6 times a veteran's aim error (`aimMul`; a regular's 1.35), takes enemies in 1.35 times as slowly, turns on a hand 0.7 times as quick, reads a target's motion three times as slowly, under-leads (0.8 of the lead, give or take 0.3), holds each tap 1.8 times as long and waits half as long on its bloom, reads a held angle a third of the time, takes fights 0.3 worse on the odds, flanks 0.4 times as often, notices someone come round its cover on a think only 35% of the time and lets half of its abilities' moments pass. The knobs run in a line from the rookie row (the rookie band's middle) to the regular row (the regular band's middle) to the veteran row, which is the identity: veterans are the bots as they were. On the aim range (`bench-bots.ts --aim-range`, one bot against a human who never fires back, 30 seeds) a rookie misses 1.16 to 1.45 times as many rounds as a veteran, 1.32 times on average over four ranges (48% against 33% at 250 px standing, 75% against 55% strafing; a regular 38% and 63%). In duels (`--duels`, the scripted human against one bot at a time, 30 seeds of 2 minutes) his K/D is 8.1 against rookies, 4.9 against regulars, 3.6 against veterans (the bots as they were) and 4.3 against the mixed draw, with 446, 544, 581 and 566 damage taken a minute. Name plates show no skill: a room should read as a lobby of people. The duel, flank, freeze, dodge, balance and zombie-curve benches play every bot as a veteran, so they measure what they did.
  - A bot in cover re-checks it on every think against each enemy it can see (`coverBlown` in `intent.ts`): an enemy with a line into its hiding spot sends it to cover from him, a rusher in reach at him, or into a fight from where it stands. `tick.ts` wakes a bot the tick an enemy in its view box gets a line on it, and a bot answers the enemy hitting it or firing its way before the one it was fighting. `node scripts/bench-flank.ts` measures how fast a bot faces, fires back and moves when a human comes round its cover.

## Performance
`node scripts/bench-tick.ts [mode] [maps] [humans] [players] [seconds]` runs a full room in one process the way `room.ts` ticks it and prints ms per tick split into bot think, step, snapshot build and encode, with snapshot bytes per player. The October 2026 pass, 1 person and 17 bots on airbase, wasteland and embassy (4-core box, other loads running, so medians):

- **Step**: about 1.1 to 0.7 ms a tick. Swing doors read their shut leaves from a cache (built once per door, with a box to skip far bodies), and who is alive or walking is read once for all doors. `coverRects` and `solidRects` are kept per world and handed out again while walls, crates, barrels, props and buildings are unchanged, rather than a fresh 150 to 700-wall copy for every player's move. `w.walls` keeps its array while no wall expires. A rewound shot uses the walls array its history frames share, rather than a deduplicated copy.
- **Encode**: about 0.13 to 0.08 ms per player a tick. Numbers are rounded into a copy that plain `JSON.stringify` writes (a replacer function is three times slower). Sticky fields are compared with what was last sent in place, rather than stringified every tick. The output is the same to the byte (`test/wire-encoder.test.ts`).
- **Whole tick**: median about 2.0 to 1.5 ms with 1 person (about 6% to 4.5% of a core), and 3.0 to 2.0 ms with 8. A room with no humans does not tick (4 empty rooms are under 1% of a core in all).
- **Bytes**: about 1.1 KB a snapshot, 33-40 KB/s per player. `SKIRMISH_DEFLATE=1` turns on permessage-deflate, which brings that to 4-6 KB/s for about 1% of a core per player. It is off by default: when the event loop is saturated (the e2e test's 8x speed), sockets waiting on zlib build a backlog, and the stall rule cuts them off. `measure-bandwidth.ts` prints the bytes that cross the socket.
- **Client**: `PROFILE=<file> DPR=3 node .claude/skills/verify/scripts/frametime.ts "$RUN" 15 852 393` also records a CPU profile and logs the main thread's top self time and GC pauses. In headless Chrome without a GPU, most frame time is raster. In JS, the CPU fallback for the night light (`drawNightFx`, whose two full-view composites stand in for the WebGL lighting pass) and blurred loose shadows lead. Snapshot decode is about 0.1%, and GC runs 1 to 2.5 short pauses a second. `matchMedia` queries made several times a frame (about 3.4 ms/s) are now made once.

```bash
docker run -p 8080:8080 -v skirmish-data:/data tinwar
```

The server limits WebSocket connections, login attempts and new guest profiles per IP address. Behind a reverse proxy every player arrives from the proxy's address, so they all share one limit. Set `TRUST_PROXY=1` to key the limits on the last `X-Forwarded-For` address instead, which is the one your proxy appends. Enable it only when exactly one proxy you control sits in front of the server, because clients can put any value they like at the front of that header.

```bash
docker run -p 8080:8080 -e TRUST_PROXY=1 -v skirmish-data:/data tinwar
```

### Fly.io

`fly.toml` runs the Docker image (its volume, `skirmish_data`, keeps the name it was created under) on one always-on machine with a volume at `/data`, `TRUST_PROXY=1` (Fly's edge is the one proxy in front), and a `/healthz` check. Keep it to one machine: rooms live in memory and the accounts file is not shared between machines.

```bash
fly launch --no-deploy --copy-config --name <your-app-name>
```

```bash
fly volumes create skirmish_data --size 1 --region iad
```

```bash
fly deploy
```

Password-reset email needs a mail provider and the site's public address, set as Fly secrets (they reach the server as environment variables; without them reset requests still answer the same but send nothing, and the log says email is not configured):

```bash
fly secrets set RESEND_API_KEY=re_... MAIL_FROM="Tinwar <no-reply@tinwar.io>" PUBLIC_URL=https://tinwar.io
```

`MAIL_FROM` must be on a domain verified in Resend. To use SMTP instead, set `SMTP_URL` (for example `smtps://user:pass@smtp.example.com:465`) and `MAIL_FROM` in place of `RESEND_API_KEY`. `PUBLIC_URL` is where reset links point; it is never taken from a request's Host header. Never set `DEV_MAIL_LOG` in production (the server ignores it under `NODE_ENV=production`).

Change `primary_region` and the volume region to the one nearest your players. A deploy restarts the machine and disconnects everyone, so deploy at quiet times.
